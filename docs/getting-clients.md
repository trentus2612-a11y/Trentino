## The niche you already have

OzTrek4x4, Performance Covers, Ice Blast Adelaide. All automotive. That's not a
coincidence worth ignoring — it's a specialisation forming by accident.

**"Websites for Adelaide's automotive and trade businesses"** beats "websites for
Adelaide businesses" because trades refer within trades, you already understand
the problems (quoting, configurators, "price without ringing"), and a detailer
looking at your work sees three businesses like theirs rather than three
strangers.

Aim outreach at: detailers, mechanics, mobile mechanics, tyre shops, window
tinters, 4x4 fitters, caravan and camping suppliers, panel beaters, auto
electricians, upholsterers, dyno/performance shops.

## The spec mockup — the play that actually works

Most outreach is ignored because it asks for something. This one gives something.

1. **Find ten.** Drive your area. Note automotive businesses with no website, or
   one that clearly hasn't been touched in a decade. Facebook-page-only businesses
   are the best prospects of all — they already believe in being online.
2. **Pick the best one.** Best = real business, visibly busy, bad web presence.
3. **Spend an hour.** Build a rough homepage. Their name, their photos (pull from
   their Facebook), their actual services. It does not need to be finished.
4. **Send it.** In person beats email; email beats a DM.

You can build a Starter in under a week, so one speculative hour against a
$349–$1,800 job is sound maths. Do this once a week and you will not be short
of work.

### In person

> Gday — I build websites for automotive businesses around Adelaide. I had a look
> at yours and made a start on what it could be. No charge, no obligation, just
> thought it'd be easier to show you than describe it. Have a look when you get
> a minute — trentino-adelaide.netlify.app

### Referral ask (send to all three clients now)

> Glad it's doing the job. If you know anyone in the trade whose website is
> embarrassing them, I've got room for one or two more this month at the lower
> rate. Happy for you to pass my number on.

Specific beats vague. "Anyone in the trade whose website is embarrassing them"
gets a name; "let me know if you hear of anything" gets nothing.

## When they say "how much?"

Send them to the estimator rather than quoting on the spot:
`trentino-adelaide.netlify.app/#estimate`

They get a number immediately, and the enquiry arrives already scoped. Quoting
in a text message is how you end up building a $1,500 site for $349.

## Don't bother with

- Paid ads before you have a Google Business Profile. You'd be paying for traffic
  you could get free.
- Instagram for a B2B trade service. Your buyers are not browsing it for a web
  developer.
- Cold-calling a list you bought.
- Lowering the price further. $349 is already a portfolio price. **Raise it after
  two more clients.**
